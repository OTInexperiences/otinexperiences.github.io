I have just finished my latest OT turning. It is a small trinket box 190mm diameter and 70mm high to the lid.  The main box is made from rippled sycamore, the knob on the lid is blackwood and the base of this is some type of mahogony which I cut from a reclaimed piece of wood.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/01f4436d8f-IMG_0285.jpg' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-1061" height="300" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/0e6fefd8bc-IMG_0285-295x300.jpg' | relative_url }}" title="IMG_0285" width="295"/></a>

The outside is covered with 8 spiral cuts made using the g code program I have described elsewhere on this site. Because it is rippled the grain on the side was very wild and tore in places as I cut the spirals. So I sprayed the sides with black laquer and then over covered with some gilding wax and finished with a couple of layers of acrylic varnish. I had already made the knob for a previous project (quite a while ago)  that did not materialise so I thought I would use it here waste not want not!!  It is not solid blackwood as I cut it in two halves and hollowed out before regluing back together and finished turning the shape.  I originally intended to cut a spiral on the sphere but having forgotted the wall thickness decided against it.

I cut a five spirals on the inside of the box lid starting at 10mm radius, with my cutting frame running vertically. I quite like how the initial cuts come together to make a pentagon in the centre. I cut a circle at the centre to complete this figure. I then filled the spirals with gilding wax to slightly hilight them above the grain and to match the theme of the box.  I also cut 5 spirals inside the box on the base with the cutting frame running horizontally to see what the effect would be. I was left with a small figure comprising of five raised petals at the centre.

<a href="http://otinexperiences.co.uk/blog/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/IMG_0292.jpg"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-1062" height="300" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="IMG_0292" width="242"/></a>

Originally, I left the actual bottom of the box plain turned but I revised this by adding a pewter insert on which I cut 8 spirals to match those cut on the side of the box.
