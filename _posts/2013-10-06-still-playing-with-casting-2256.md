Trying to refine my techniques for making a casting mold.
<ol>
<li>Start by turning blank perfectly flat. Check face flatness with the edge of a ruler. When satisfied sand face down to 320 grit and check again finally seal with sanding sealer.</li>
<li>Mount blank centrally on the machine. I use a cone mounted in a separate chuck and then move the cutter so it is in line with the point of the cone. Then remove this and replace with chuck holding the blank.</li>
<li>Use a dial indicator to check that tool moves perfectly parallel to face of blank by taking a reading at the edge and moving tool to centre. Rotate either headstock or tool-post until there is little difference between the two readings.  For this purpose I am happy to get the difference to be less than 0.2 mm.</li>
<li>Place indicator at edge of blank again and adjust leveling chuck so that difference in a complete 360 degree rotation is small (again less than 0.2mm).</li>
<li>Cut pattern on blank.</li>
<li>Remove back to wood lathe and sand down to 1200 grit then seal again. Remove all dust from surface of blank.</li>
<li>Cast mold in silicone rubber. I use a non stick pie-dish (£1 from pound-shop) place blank in pie-dish and cover with silicone rubber.</li>
<li>Leave for 24 hours to cure then remove blank to reveal mold.</li>
<li>Test by taking a plaster of Paris casting.</li>
</ol>

The plaster cast above shows a few defects where the plaster did not quite fill correctly. Also you can see a slight fault. There was a small split in the base which I filled with superglue and sawdust. I thought I had sanded down correctly but the final cast shows otherwise. I have learnt that you have to be super-super careful.
