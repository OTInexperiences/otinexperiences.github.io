Despite having a problem with insufficient pewter for the first fill, I bought some more and completed the platter. Unfortunately you can see where the two fills met look at the bottom right hand corner. The wood is oak and about 10.5 inches (270mm) in diameter.

<a href="http://otinexperiences.co.uk/blog/wp-content/uploads/2010/11/img_0020-bowl3.jpg"><img alt="" class="size-large wp-image-753 alignnone" height="287" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="img_0020 (bowl3)" width="368"/></a>
<p style="text-align: left;">I wanted the pattern to be visible on both sides of the platter so I cut the pattern to within 2mm of the bottom, filled with pewter and skimmed the bottom back to reveal the pattern. I had to use copious amounts of super glue along the edges where the pewter and wood meet to stabilize the platter.</p>
<p style="text-align: left;">I sanded down to 240 grit with little problem but found that with grits below 240 the pewter dust bled into the grain of the wood despite sealing very carefully with sanding sealer. The effect as you see in the image above is to make the grain stand out. I tried several times to sand down to 400 grit but the wood became very dirty as the smaller particles of pewter filled smaller voids in the wood. I skimmed the wood back 3 times trying to avoid this.So in future I need to find something to fill the wood grain before sanding below 240 grit or use a harder wood. I think this would look really great done in black-wood but at present that would be a little to costly for me to produce.</p>
