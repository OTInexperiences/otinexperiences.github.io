<p style="text-align: left;">Whilst cutting a pattern on the base of a box recently, I realized that the head on my cutting frame was too large, so time for version 2. I kept the original brass cutter holder but skimmed it down a little and re machined its shaft to 9mm diameter so I could use some smaller bearings which have an inside diameter of 9mm, 17mm outside diameter and 5mm wide.  I remade the casing from a small piece of aluminium and bored a hole 17 wide and 15mm deep enough to hold three bearings.  My new head now uses triple bearings which should help cut down vibration. I also machined a new matching brass pulley wheel to replace the larger aluminium original.</p>

The flywheel connected to my 1/2 hp motor is 100mm diameter, the pulley attached to the cutter is 20 mm diameter which gives my cutter a maximum speed of approx 5 times the motor speed of 1400 or approx 7000 rpm. I can run the motor up to 2800 rpm by ramping the controller frequency to 100 hz which would give my cutter  a theoretical top speed of 14000 rpm.  Sticking at  7000 for the time being.

The image below shows the head fixed onto a small 100mm extension to allow me to reach into the bottom of boxes etc. I can make the extension as large as I want by adding on other extension pieces. Of course vibration will become a problem with longer lengths.
