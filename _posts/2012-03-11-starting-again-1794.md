This last week I have managed to get into the shed, the first time for quite a while. I made a small box out of rippled sycamore. Whilst hollowing the box I cut through the bottom!!! This is the first time ever that I have done this. So rather than throw eveything away I decided to use the bit I had cut off for the lid as a new base for the box. I first cut of the ruined base leaving me with a cylinder whoose inside I finished and sanded. Before I glued the base to the cylinder, I sanded and polished it.

I decided at this point that I would add a decorative ring between the base and the cylinder so I laminated a thin ring out of eight pieces of ebony and glued that onto the cylinder. When dry I turned the ring so it was the same internal diameter as the cylinder and faced it so it was flat, finishing of by sanding. I then glued the base to the ebony ring. As the piece for the base was 20mm thick I now had an ebony ring 20mm up from the bottom of the box. So I decided to add a mating ring 20mm down from the top. So I cut the cylinder again and glued on a second ring. I finished off by facing everything to the same outside diameter, sanding and polishing.

My original idea was to cut a pattern between the two ebony bands, but after polishing I decided to leave the wood untouched, so that now left me with a lid to make. I thought that a black lid would match the bands on the box side, but I did not have a piece of ebony large enough so I decided to cut the top from a different wood and spray it with ebony laquer. I wanted to cut a pattern in the lid so I chose a piece of lime plank about 25mm thick, as I thought that it would hold the detail. I started by drilling a 40mm recess in the lime to be used to hold the wood on the chuck. After turning to a diameter slightly larger than the box body, I faced it flat and then cut a bevel starting about 5mm from the centre so that the slope was 10mm in depth at the edge.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/3926feffe9-top2.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1795" height="98" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/3926feffe9-top2.png' | relative_url }}" title="top2" width="200"/></a>I then transferred this to my rose engine and cut a series of twisted flutes in the top. Using a vertical cutting frame I set the depth of cut to 0.5mm. My simple<a href="{{ '/software/g-code-examples/cutting-flutes-on-a-box-lid/' | relative_url }}" title="Cutting flutes on a box lid"> hand coded gcode program</a> just moves the work past the cutter starting from 5mm from the centre, and simultaneously moves the work towards the cutter to account for the slope whilst rotating the work slightly. This resulted in a series of twisted flutes cut into the top of the box. When complete the top was sprayed with black laquer.

I turned a finial out of boxwood to act as a knob.
