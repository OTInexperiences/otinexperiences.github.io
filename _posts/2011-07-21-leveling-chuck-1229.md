I have just made a simple leveling chuck from a small faceplate I bought.  The faceplate has 6 holes each 6mm diameter and equidistant, around the faceplates periphery. I used these to provide the leveling action.  I first drilled out three holes (every other hole) and tapped for an 8mm thread.  I then took three 8mm  cap head screws and filed the head to a 10mm square so as to fit a spanner, these would be the leveling adjustment screws.

Now I utilised a small M33 x 3.5 spindle stub I was not using. I bought this quite some time ago from Trent Bosch. I had previously bought one of his <a href="http://www.trentbosch.com/catalog/product_info.php?cPath=24&amp;products_id=89">carving stands</a> and the boss it uses proved to be just what I wanted (I have an M33 x3.5 thread on my lathe and have not really the capacity to machine a thread of that pitch). So I emailed Trent and asked if he would sell me just the boss and he kindly agreed (I also bought one of his super tool handles at the same time).

I machined the boss to take out most of its thickness till all I had left of the base of the boss was a flat piece 5mm thick.  I machined a matching recess 5mm deep in a piece of aluminium plate 12mm thick which I turned to a disc whose diameter was a little larger than that of my faceplate. (The aluminium was a scrap piece I had so contained several holes machined for a previous project you can see these on the photo below).

I then bolted the boss into the recess I had machined with five equi-spaced 5mm machine screws. Also in this disc I drilled and tapped three 6mm diameter holes to exactly match the 3 untapped holes in my faceplate.

<a href="http://otinexperiences.co.uk/blog/wp-content/uploads/2011/07/IMG_0388.jpg"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-1231" height="225" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="IMG_0388" width="300"/></a>

Three 6mm diameter cap head screws are placed in the untapped holes in the faceplate and threaded onto the spindle thread carrier plate. These are tightened so as to pull the carrier plate towards the faceplate. The three 8mm cap head bolts are threaded into the faceplate with their heads facing towards the carrier plate so as you unscrew them they will tend to push the carrier plate away from the faceplate.

It is the tension between both sets of bolts that allows us to make minute adjustments to the position of the carrier plate and thus the angle of the spindle which enables us to compensate for any small axial discrepancies.

By the way the thick aluminium washer you see in the photo above is there to make up the difference between the short depth of thread on my small chuck and the length of the spindle thread. It is removed when my main chuck is placed on the spindle.

To test the leveling capability I used my cole jaws which are 16 inches in diameter. After fiddling with the chuck I managed to adjust it so that the discrepancy was less than 0.1mm at the edge of the jaws (8 inches from the centre of the spindle). I am sure that if I spent longer I would achieve a better result. As it is I wanted the chuck to be able to level a pewter disc only 4-5 inches in diameter so I am fairly confident in its ability to do this.
