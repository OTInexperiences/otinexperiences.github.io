I have just completed my second patterned bowl.  I used a 12 inches diameter block of rippled sycamore. Well in fact I used two!! I chose the simple hypo-cycloid pattern because the ripple effect was very strong down two sides of the bowl and I wanted something that would not overwhelm the natural beauty of the wood.
<p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="img_0210" class="ngg-singlepic ngg-none aligncenter" height="336" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" width="448"/></p>
Cutting the pattern was not a problem but filling it with resin was.  For my first attempt I soaked the piece in food safe finishing oil and left it for a day. Then I used black ink to colour the path. Silly thing to do as the ink  just bled into the wood.  So I turned off the pattern back to clean wood and re-cut. Then treated the bowls surface with one coat of sanding sealer. Instead of using ink I bought some black additive to colour my resin and refilled the pattern. I let the resin fill the channel and overflow a little. Big mistake again as the colour still bled into the wood (see picture in gallery).  So now I tried to bleach the wood with ordinary household bleach, this didn't  do anything except darken the wood. So onto second attempt.

Again cutting pattern was straightforward. This time two coats of sanding sealer. I used the same black colour additive to the resin but dribbled it slowly directly into the grooves and only allowed it to fill to the top of the groove. This seemed to work but at one point I was too careless and let a blob of resin drop onto the surface of the bowl and even though I wiped it off immediately I wasn't fast enough as  the colour still bled into the surface at that spot. Turned as much of the surface off as I dared but you can still see the effects faintly.  However decided to live with it but next time I try this with rippled sycamore I think I will have to totally finish and wax the surface before cutting the pattern, and even then stick sellotape to seal it further.

So two good pieces of wood turned into one ok-ish bowl.

One good thing did come out of this I now know what to do with the left over resin.  I am going to cut a pattern on it and  use it to fill in the bases of my bowls. See  image below.

<img alt="bowlbase" class="ngg-singlepic ngg-none" height="209" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" width="197"/>


I took  a piece of scrap wood and turned a recess for my chuck.  Once mounted on the chuck I used my parting tool to  cut  out a recess to the diameter  I required  60mm in this case.  Now took the chuck off the lathe and placed so wood surface was level and filled the recess with my spare resin.  Once the resin hardened I placed the chuck on my rose machine and cut out the pattern using a 1mm cutter and removed from the wood.  I then painted the pattern with gold paint and when dry sanded the resin disk so that only grooves were gold.  I then super-glued the disc into the bottom of my finished bowl making sure that the depth was such that the disc surface was a few mm below the foot.  Finally I poured a covering coat of the crystal clear resin over the disc to hold in place and give a nice shiny finish. By the way the pattern on the base is an epicycloid which I thought was suitable because in a sense its the opposite of the hypo-cycloid pattern on the top.
