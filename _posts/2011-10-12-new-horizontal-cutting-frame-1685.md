Just after cutting the second spiral I noticed that my cutting frame had gone a bit wobbley.  Obviously rotating the spindle at approx 6000 rpm in a short aluminium sleeve even using three bearings, is not the way to go. So I started to make a more robust one from a 25mm (1 inch ) square section piece of mild steel bar.

This time the shaft was also machined from mild steel as I had run out of brass bar. The shaft was machined to 9mm diameter to fit the roller bearings I recycled from the previous frame. The end holding the cutter is 16mm diameter with a 6mm threaded portion at the opposite end.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/48b9009bf4-Spindle.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-1687" height="300" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/e599b38c38-Spindle-257x300.png' | relative_url }}" title="Spindle" width="257"/></a>

As you see this time I have used 5 minature roller bearings around the shaft, each 9mm bore and 18 mm outer diameter and 5mm thick. I machined a small pulley 25mm diameter to just fit the size of the housing and threaded it with a 6mm bore.  The pulley is screwed to a light fit with no play, on the minature thrust bearing and locked off with a 6mm nut.

I cut a second piece of bar and milled a 90 degree V in one end. I then got someone to weld a corner of the spindle body into this V in order to make a T shape as in the image below.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/ccf02f59b8-ctframe1.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-1689" height="165" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/215e042431-ctframe1-300x165.png' | relative_url }}" title="ctframe1" width="300"/></a><a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/ccf02f59b8-ctframe1.png' | relative_url }}">
</a>

After welding I placed the frame on my mill and ran a light cut over the bottom of the horizontal bar in order to true it up so that the spindle was truly perpendicular to base of the T piece. Finally to finish I milled off the remaining three corners of the spindle body and drilled and threaded a hole on the end of the frame to hold the pulleys and also drilled a hole to allow to allow the frame to be bolted to its supporting column.
