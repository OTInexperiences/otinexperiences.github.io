After my earlier attempt at infilling with pewter I had some left over. So I thought that it would be nice to create some small medallions that I could decorate and use to infill the chucking recess left after finishing my bowls, instead of the usual method of turning the recess away.

So I started by making a simple mould by turning flat a scrap piece of pine I have had lying about the workshop and adding a rim made from mdf.

So I put the mould still held in my small chuck on my simple leveling table (used when pouring resin casts), heated the pewter and filled the mould with molten pewter.

<figure class="wp-recovered-caption">
<a href="http://otinexperiences.co.uk/blog/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/IMG_0308.jpg"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-1094" height="225" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="IMG_0308" width="300"/></a>
<figcaption>casting setup</figcaption>
</figure>

I was very surprised and a little worried when, as it cooled, the surface of the pewter heaved as trapped steam tried to escape. It looked just like one of those volcanic mud pools.  When finally solidified the result was a chunk of pewter all pitted on the underside and containing small interior bubbles as I found out later when trimming the surface.

The previous time I had used the pewter to fill what was in effect a narrow (approx 5mm wide) channel there had been no issue with bubbling and I guess I thought that this would be the case with a larger mould. Now I know that all wood has a moisture content but the wood I used had been lying in a dry environment for several years and I assumed that there would be little steam, I was very wrong.

So back to the drawing board for mould version 2 which I made  by milling out a circle in a small piece of aluminium 12mm thick and then attaching a base plate of aluminium 5mm thick. This time everything worked fine with no bubbling whatsoever.  So the moral is do not use wood for significantly large moulds.
