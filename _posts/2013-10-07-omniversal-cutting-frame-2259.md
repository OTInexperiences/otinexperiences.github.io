The latest SOT bulletin arrived today and I was particularly interested in the article by John Edwards called the Omniversal cutting frame based on an idea by Walter Wenninga. I had been wondering for a while how I could get a small radial cut and lo and behold John presented a solution. Looking at Photo 3 in his article I thought that I had most of the bits to replicate that. I could use a small boring bar and my drilling frame. I needed to turn the base of the boring bar to 9mm diameter as that is the largest collet size I have for the drilling frame. Also I had to add some support to allow me to mount the pulleys I normally use on my standard cutting frame to enable me to use my overhead drive. Anyway, I spent an afternoon in the workshop and the results are shown below.

The frame can be rotated through any angle between zero and 90 degrees in the ZY plane (where the Z axis is along the headstock). There is no way of accurately establishing this angle, I will use a digital angle gauge to do this.

<figure class="wp-recovered-caption">

The view above shows that the frame is held by a single bolt to my vertical slide and so can also be rotated in the XY plane. Therefore I have covered two of the three suggested developments John made in his article.

I will try it out tomorrow. The length of the boring bar is a bit long so there may be some vibration we shall see. I am prepared to sacrifice this boring bar from my set by re-machining it as they are easy to get hold of on eBay. I will have to look for a way of accurately sharpening the carbide tip brazed onto the end of the bar.
