I have spent the last few days building a sphere turning jig. Its been on my to-do list for quite a while. I have designed it to handle spheres up to 80mm radius, but am thinking that if I machine the end-cap to match the slide I can bump this up to 100mm radius.

I machined the base so that the rotation axis sits exactly at the centre of the bed bars on my wood-turning lathe. The spindle on which the cutter arm rotates is made from a piece of 16mm diameter hardened linear rail which is 155mm long. This sits in a brass bearing which is an exact fit to the bar. The bearing is 60mm long in order to minimize wobble.

An aluminium arm sits on the spacer and this arm has a machined profile to carry a tool post whose position is determined by a piece of M8 screwed rod with 1mm pitch.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/be02521ad0-slide1.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="slide" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2338" height="126" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/09703f8747-slide1-300x126.png' | relative_url }}" width="300"/></a>

Once the correct radius has been set the tool post can be locked.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/8d7119acf1-slide11.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="slide1" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2339" height="112" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/ca25aace63-slide11-300x112.png' | relative_url }}" width="300"/></a>

The tool post holds a machine insert set on a piece of  steel.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/b8597adc1e-5-IMG_0017.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="5-IMG_0017" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2331" height="286" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/5ee96b7c1b-5-IMG_0017-300x286.png' | relative_url }}" width="300"/></a>instead of machining the shape to fit the insert directly on the top of the tool post. This gives some adjustment to the position of the cutting point. In fact the cutting point can be placed on the back side of the tool-post in order to cut concave profiles. The insert height is exactly at the height of the centre of my lathe spindle.
