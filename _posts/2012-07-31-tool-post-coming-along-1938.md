Yesterday I completed my add-on Y slide for the tool post. As you see it is attached to the Myford slide I acquired recently.  The stepper is not wired up yet as I currently have only a 4 axis breakout board etc. I will have to upgrade my controller to 5 axis for it to be useful.

<figure class="wp-recovered-caption">
<img alt="yslide1" class="ngg-singlepic ngg-center" height="360" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/60ecb0ee15-yslide1.png' | relative_url }}" width="284"/> General view of Y slide with cutting frame attached
</figure>

For the time being I can try cutting patterns as a linear engine by utilizing the driver from another axis, as all I require are X Y and Z for this task. I ran the cutting frame at full rpm which is about 6000+ with an out of balance cutter 30 mm long, and the vibration was noticeably much reduced as I had hoped.

So now I have two options, I can hold the cutting frame either directly on the Myford slide or alternatively on this new slide.  I had originally thought that the Y slide would be an extra but I am now considering leaving it on permanently.

<figure class="wp-recovered-caption">
<img alt="img_0285" class="ngg-singlepic ngg-center" height="360" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/be19fb6b02-img_0285.png' | relative_url }}" width="344"/> Rear view of slide
</figure>

I also utilized the shield taken off my mill as I am more worried about a small sharp cutter held in a slot by a grub screw and rotating at 5-6000 rpm than I am of a milling cutter rotating at 5-800 rpm and held in a proper collet.

To complete it I just need to machine a correctly sized spacer to go between the cutting frame itself and the final pulleys . The images above show me using two bits of material with the right size hole.
