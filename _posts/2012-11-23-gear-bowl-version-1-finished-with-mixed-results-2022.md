Last week I finished the gear bowl but there were problems and it needs improvement.

Whilst it may not be apparent from the image above, the sets of gears are covered with a resin top which makes the surface of the bowl flat. And it was this that is the real cause of concern and it is a fault of my own making. In my haste to complete the bowl (in time for a competition) I did not leave the resin to dry long enough, It felt hard when I started the final finishing but it soon became apparent that it was not, and this left a poor surface. I need to skim off the surface and cover again with a shell coat and it should be fine. Unfortunately I could not do this before the competition so that was my entry down the drain. I will strive for better in version 2 and leave a sufficiently long drying time. I ran short of time because the bowl itself proved challenging (to me anyway) to make and several approaches had to be taken before I had worked out a way of proceeding.

The idea is that just beneath the lip of the bowl there is a rim and that when you spin the rim the gears turn. The bowl is sycamore and the gears are made from Boxwood,  Pau Amarello and Walnut. The rotating rim is also made from walnut. In this version the bowl itself is small, just large enough to take a 2 inches diameter candle. So its main purpose would be a candle holder. The gears actually do rotate as you spin the edge.
