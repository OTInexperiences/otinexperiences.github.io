Just a few comments about the build process.
<ol>
<li>Firstly the kit you receive is complete, everything is provided.</li>
<li>You have to be patient, I found that it took me several days to complete the mechanical build. You definitely will need a small needle file to ensure that some parts fit together.</li>
<li>Beware the acrylic components are very brittle. I must admit that one of the lugs locating the X axis on the X-axis motor mount snapped off as I forced it a little too much (should have used a file there!). This hasn't seemed to have made much difference.</li>
<li>The electronic wiring is fairly straightforward as mostly pre-wired looms are provided. I did have problems connecting all the single wire crimp connections to the wires and inserting them into the requisite terminal blocks. I have a hobby crimping tool but  this was unsuitable for crimping the single wires onto the terminal connectors. I tried crimping the wires to the connectors using flat nosed pliers but not with any success,when commissioning I found that connections to both the hot-end thermistor and the heated bed thermistor were open circuit. So I soldered both these connections and this solved the problem.</li>
<li>There was a problem with the Duet board connecting to my pc, but this was quickly replaced by RepRapPro and the new board works fine. I am grateful for the <strong>excellent support provided by the company</strong>.</li>
<li>The software running the printer seems quite secure and seems to function correctly although as you would expect some bugs need ironing out. Here there are a couple of very helpful members on the forum who have already made significant contributions to the improvement of this software.</li>
<li>Do not expect perfect prints as soon as you start. It took me a couple of attempts even to get the commissioning print correct. As with all things it will take time  and mistakes in order to tune the printer and gain the experience to enable you and me to produce good quality output.</li>
<li>Regarding the build time. If I discount the problems with the duet board, it took me about 2 weeks to build and commission the printer.  A couple of years ago I designed and built my cnc based rose engine buying in common parts and machining all others from scratch. That took me 8 months+ so it seems to me that getting the RepRapPro kit is a fast and efficient way of getting into 3D printing.</li>
<li>I think that the kit is an excellent starting point. It is my personal opinion that the Omerod design is superior to earlier versions simply because of its use of aluminium section. I am sure that it will evolve into an even better printer in the future.</li>
<li>Overall I did swear a few times but in general found that the build went fairly smoothly. If you look at a couple of users on the forum you could get the wrong impression about the kit. It is a hobbyist kit and as a new development has some rough edges. Some people seem to have expected a fully professional machine of the £10,000+ variety for the money. It is not that, it is for the hobbyist and seems to be doing a creditable job here.</li>
</ol>
