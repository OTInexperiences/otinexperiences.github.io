In order to do this I had to make a smaller cutting frame as the one I use was too large to fit inside a 146mm diameter circle which was the size I wanted to try. There are many things wrong with the internal gear shown below.  But it was a quick first attempt to check that my new cutting head worked .

<h2>The Cutting Head</h2>
There were two objectives in mind when I made my cutting head, firstly I was not going to buy any material for it as I did not want to have to wait the couple of days it would take to get stuff delivered. And secondly I wanted it to hold the cutters I had made already, so this meant that the size of the slot used to hold the cutter had to be 12mm by 6mm wide.

I had run out of bearings so I first thought I would make a head that ran on hardened points. The I had the idea  to use a single ball bearing as the bearing surface. I had bought some individual 4mm ball bearings to have a go at making a lazy Susan type bearing.

So I came up with the idea below. The rotating head would sit between two single ball bearings which would in turn locate onto the end of two pieces of threaded rod each locked by a nut. Adjusting the thread would eliminate any vertical play in the cutting frame.

When I tried it it worked well I just do not know how robust this cutting frame will turn out to be. But it is simple to make, you just need to use a centre drill to make the location for the ball at each end of the cutter barrel.
<h2>Holding the blank for cutting</h2>
I started out by partially hollowing out a solid wood blank to the required diameter. This was then held on my mandrel for the actual gear cutting. The g code program is exactly the same as for cutting an external gear except that the depth of cut for the in-feed will be in the opposite direction, that is towards the outside of the blank.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/5d789bdb4a-02-IMG_0002.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2005" height="226" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/ee5fa41206-02-IMG_0002-300x226.png' | relative_url }}" title="02-IMG_0002" width="300"/></a>

After cutting the blank was returned to my wood lathe where the outside and face were  trued up and then a slice of wood that made the gear was parted off.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/1e1468e5b9-09-IMG_0009.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2010" height="226" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/45d66bd8b5-09-IMG_0009-300x226.png' | relative_url }}" title="09-IMG_0009" width="300"/></a>

I need to find a better way of holding the gear blanks. I would like to cut the gear on a slice whose thickness represented the final depth of the gear. This would allow me to make up a gear blank from segments rather that having to hollow out a solid piece of wood. My initial thoughts are to use my cole jaws and hold the slice away from the jaws using spacers.
