<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/60f9362bdb-levelling-plate1.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="levelling-plate1" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2498" height="300" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/7b06e86696-levelling-plate1-177x300.png' | relative_url }}" width="177"/></a>I needed a chuck to hold small flat items on my headstock perfectly perpendicular to etching cutter held in my tool post. So I decided to make a purpose built item. I started with a bought 150mm faceplate that has an M33x1.5 thread to suit my headstock. This faceplate has several existing holes to allow items to be screwed to the faceplate. I drilled these holes out and tapped them with an  M8 thread.  This enabled me to bolt a 200mm square by 6mm thick piece of flat aluminium plate to the faceplate.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/658585a6de-levelling-plate-1.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="levelling plate-1" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2507" height="223" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/1365b80c55-levelling-plate-1-300x223.png' | relative_url }}" width="300"/></a>

To this lower plate, an upper plate of the same size is attached at three points using three 6mm bolts that are firmly bolted to the upper plate.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/dc63503870-leveling-plate-assembly.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="leveling plate assembly" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2509" height="221" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/e7330b9310-leveling-plate-assembly-300x221.png' | relative_url }}" width="300"/></a>

Six knurled brass nuts tapped at M6 and shown shaded blue above, allow the distance between the fixed lower plate and the movable upper plate to be adjusted at the three attachment points.  By this means the position of the upper plate may be adjusted until it is perfectly perpendicular to the tool. Finally a wooden sacrificial surface is attached to the upper plate.
