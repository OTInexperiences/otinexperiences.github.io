<h2>Idea</h2>
I was talking to Jerry Marlow at a recent meeting of my <a href="http://evwa.freei.me/" target="_blank">wood turning club</a> about the idea of making a  rose engine add on for a small lathe. Jerry suggested the idea of holding the rosettes on a shaft with an M2 taper as this is the usual taper size used on the main shafts on wood turning lathes here in the UK. At the time we were envisioning a setup to be used on a small lathe such as the <a href="http://www.axminster.co.uk/axminster-axminster-awvsl-woodturning-lathe-prod781208/http://" target="_blank">Axminster AWVSL</a> or similar machines made by other manufacturers.

The idea being that the Index plate plus one or more rosettes plus a chuck adapter would be held on a boss attached to the M2 the tapered  shaft, so that you would have something like the following.

Looking for a tapered item I noticed that <a href="http://www.chronos.ltd.uk/acatalog/index.html" target="_blank">Chronos</a> had a suitable product which is  NEW 2MT BLANK END ARBOR WITH 28MM END  (Ref: BEA10N) so I bought a couple to try.

The draw-bar would go through the hollow shaft of the lathe and terminate in a pulley.

<figure class="wp-recovered-caption">
<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/a49d9dff7d-headstock.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="size-medium wp-image-1837" height="251" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/7667dcdd5b-headstock-300x251.png' | relative_url }}" title="headstock" width="300"/></a>
<figcaption>Draw-bar holds pulley and rosette arrangement to shaft</figcaption>

The rosette would rub against a frame that would move horizontally and run on linear rail and bearings which are clamped to the bed bars of the lathe .

<figure class="wp-recovered-caption">
<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/f9a13bcbcf-slider1.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="size-medium wp-image-1839" height="295" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/e282b1e2d6-slider1-300x295.png' | relative_url }}" title="slider1" width="300"/></a>

The top of the frame would be covered by a platform holding the cutting frame on an XY table or similar arrangement.
<h2>My build</h2>
I decided to have a go at making this add on for my own lathe which is rather larger than one mentioned above and also it has a solid shaft, so the method of holding the taper in the shaft will need to be modified.  I have not finalized the modification just yet but am thinking of using a faceplate as the anchoring mechanism (as shown in the image below).

I already had two pieces of  25mm diameter linear rail each 300mm long and two pillow block bearings, so I bought two more bearings and some aluminum to make the frame.  I wanted to make this frame beefier than my previous computerized build so I bought some solid aluminum bar 2 inches by 1 inch by 750 mm long for the sides of the frame.  I have probably vastly over-engineered this (and certainly similar frame on a smaller lathe would not need 25mm diameter linear rail or anything like this bar size).  But the resultant frame certainly is solid. I can hang onto one side of the frame without causing any movement at all and the frame slides easily from side to side with little friction.

The two linear rails are about 50mm apart and I originally intended to add cross members connected to each side bar in order to stiffen them up. But with the sections I am using this is not necessary as the frame is already  rigid.  In the original design the side bars would end about 50mm behind the rail furthest from the headstock.  Keeping the side bars to their full length as shown above would give me plenty of working room so I am considering keeping them as is rather than cutting them shorter.

<h2>My Rose head modifications</h2>
As I have said the main difference is that the M2 socket is in a  solid drive shaft as the motor is attached directly to the end of the shaft. So I have to find some way of anchoring the M2 arbor to stop it coming loose.  My idea is to use a screw on faceplate and attach the chuck adapter to that.

<h2>Rosette phasing</h2>
In the simplest case I can bolt the rosette directly to the faceplate, but I am hoping to try to design a more sophisticated solution than that.
<h2>Driving the shaft</h2>
My idea is to attach a large pulley to the back of the faceplate but the rest of the design is undecided as is the size of the pulley.

Finally if you are wondering about the mixed units I use the reason is that I prefer to work in mm, but I buy my aluminum from a company called <a href="http://www.forwardmetals.co.uk/" target="_blank">Forward Metals</a> who sell sections in inches but lengths in mm!
