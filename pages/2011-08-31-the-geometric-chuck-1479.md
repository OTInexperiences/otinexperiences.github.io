The geometric chuck is a mechanism added onto a lathe spindle that allows the user to cut an almost infinite number of patterns. The chuck comprises of several stages, two can be seen in the image above. Each stage comprises of its own spindle which may be offset from that of the previous stage and through the use of a gear chain may be made to rotate at a different speed and/or direction to the previous stage.  Attached to the spindle of the last stage (not shown in the image above) is a plain chuck holding the work piece.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/abd4e2c6f7-chuck-stage1.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-1485" height="262" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/dbbe9e3762-chuck-stage1-300x262.png' | relative_url }}" title="chuck-stage1" width="300"/></a>In this manner as the lathe headstock spindle rotates the work piece does not simply rotate around the spindle centre but instead rotates around an axis which is itself moving in some complex way around the headstock spindle centre.

Many millions of patterns can be generated with just two stages on the chuck. It becomes mechanically impossible to fix more that three or four stages on a lathe due to size and weight constraints. Geometric chucks with a greater number of stages were built vertically with the base stage being on the ground.

In a CNC based machine we can program a model of the mechanical chuck and use that model to calculate the path of the sequence of points in space the centre of the work piece would describe as it rotates around the lathe spindle.  In our virtual world the number of chuck stages may be unlimited and also the path of each stage may be other than the simple circular path of the purely mechanical system.
