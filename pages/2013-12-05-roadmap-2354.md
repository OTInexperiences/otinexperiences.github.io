Typically rose engines have three movements
<ol>
<li>Workpiece rotation affected by attaching the work to a rotating spindle.</li>
<li>Headstock lateral movement, controlled by the amplitude of a cam or rosette.</li>
<li>Headstock forward movement called pumping which is also controlled by the amplitude of a rosette.</li>
</ol>
In traditional mechanical machines the primary movement is the spindle rotation. Normally both the lateral and pumping movements of the headstock are synchronized with the spindle and at occur in concert with the rotation. This is achieved by rigidly fixing Rosettes or cams to the rotating spindle. These cams then push against a fixed point known as a rubber. The cam is forced to rest against the rubber by means of a spring or similar. Because the radius of the cam varies as it rotates against the fixed point, the difference between the centre of the cam and the fixed point becomes a positional movement.

We could increase the flexibility of the machine if we were to allow that each movement to occur at a different rate. We would like the speed of the cams determining lateral and pumping movements to be some, possibly different,  multiple of the speed of the spindle speed.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/e873ae45e7-motors.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="motors" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-2358" height="168" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/b509717e4a-motors-300x168.png' | relative_url }}" width="300"/></a>

We can achieve this if we separate the three axes and drive each axis by its own motor and consider using stepper or servo motors for the drive mechanism as the speed of this type of motor is dependent solely on the frequencies of the train of pulses that drive them.

The idea then is to make a manually driven rose engine that uses rosettes to for its movements. However this machine will have three separate motors each fed from a common pulse stream.
