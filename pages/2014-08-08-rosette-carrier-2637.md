Finally got around to creating the rosette carrier for my new modular rose engine. I started by making a plastic model using my 3D printer however I decided that rather than use the mode I would make an aluminium part as it would be more robust. The rosette carrier holds the rosette horizontally so that I can use the same rosette for both pumping and lateral movements. Remember that traditionally rosettes used for pumping are mounted vertically and so have to have the pattern cut on their face rather than their edge.

I opted to use two aluminium plates 200mm  long by 98mm wide  by 6mm thick. I bought a 200mm square piece off ebay, cut it in two and then milled the edges so both pieces were exactly the same size. The 98mm width allowed me to hold the plate in my mill vise which has a 100mm capacity.

The two plates are sat on five pillars each 29mm  long made from 20mm diameter aluminium.  There is a tennon 10mm diameter and 2mm deep machined onto each end of each pillar allowing it to fit into a 2mm recess machined in on both plates. Each pillar is tapped m4 at each end and is fastened to the top and bottom plate using m4 by 12mm countersunk machine screws. When assembled the distance between the plates is 25mm which is just sufficient to allow the large timing pulley to sit with no vertical movement

I chose 20mm diameter for the pillars as that was what I had in stock I am sure that 15mm or some other size will do equally as well.

<a href="{{ '/assets/recovered/5cc68ba119-fat-pillar.png' | relative_url }}"><img alt="fat-pillar" class="alignnone wp-image-2654" height="190" src="{{ '/assets/recovered/9f0e193119-fat-pillar-268x300.png' | relative_url }}" width="170"/></a>

A bronze bush is set in each plate and this holds a 10mm diameter steel shaft holding a T5 timing pulley.  This is connected to another T5 timing pulley set on the motor shaft.  The connecting belt is a 9mm wide belt with 72 teeth and the pulley sizes are such that there is a 2:1 ratio between the motor and the shaft. The motor is held in slots so that it can be moved in order to tension the belt.

Connected to the shaft above the top plate is a platter to hold either one or two rosettes.  The item shown in the photograph is a trial plastic part with 96 index positions around its circumference.

However,  I intend to change the design of the platter and machine the final version out of aluminium.  There will be some method to allow phasing in the final part but the mechanism is not yet decided.

The rosette(s) mounted on the platter will connect to the rear of the headstock via a lever mechanism. Whilst a rubber mounted on the side of the machine will provide the lateral movement.

If truly independent lateral and pumping movements are desired then a further rosette carrier may be used. Giving one dedicated to pumping and the second dedicated to a lateral movement.
