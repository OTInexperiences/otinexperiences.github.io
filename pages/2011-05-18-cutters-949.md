I have been busy lately creating some cutters for my cutting frame. I started by trying to copy some of the shapes displayed in figs 91 - 95  on page 115 of Holtzapffel volume 5 The principles &amp; practice of ornamental or complex turning. You can see these on the top row of the image below.  I started off making a V cutter with included angle of 60 deg. I was told that 120 deg was better so That is the second V cutter.  After that I made two flat cutters 5 and 10mm wide followed by two triangular ones, one right handed the other left handed. Finally I had a go at a round cutter where I thought I could use the Tormek fingernail grinding jig to get the round shape, but no it turned out to be fingernail shape. The second row shows 3 blanks that I have yet to finish.

I made these cutters out of 6mm thick gauge plate that I bought from ebay.  After cutting out the basic blank shape and filing to fit the rectangualar mortise in my cutting frame, I used my mill to cut the basic shape and then ground the clearance angles using my Tormek with the jig I made.  I hardened them by heating to a cherry red colour and then plunging them into a bath of oil (this was vegetable cooking oil kept in an old baked beans tin). I tempered them by wrapping them in foil and cooking them in the oven at 220 deg for 30 mins then turning the oven off and letting everything cool. They turned out as a nice straw colour which was reassuring. I used foil because It was the only way my wife would let them in the oven.  Finally I used the Tormek again to grind and polish each cutter.

The third row of the image shows the little rectangular aluminium insert sleeve I made that enables me to hold 5mm diameter HSS rod in the cutting frame. As the mortise in the frame is 6mm wide by 12mm long (milled with a 6mm milling cutter and files squareish) . I made the insert by first drilling a hole through a bit of ali (scrap piece with one side milled flat) with a 5mm drill. Then I cut and milled the piece roughly to shape and did the final fitting by hand filing.  I then placed the sleeve in the cutting frame and tightened the 6mm socket screw that holds the cutters, to mark the sleeve. Then back to the mill to cut a 6mm diameter slot over the mark made by the screw.This is to enable the screw to go through the sleeve and hold the HSS bar tightly.

Finally because the slot in my cutting frame is 6mm deep I can also use 6mm square HSS rod in it. The image below shows a broken 6mm HSS lathe tool resharped and used as a cutter.
