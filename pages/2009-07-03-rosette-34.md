Rosettes are thin plates of some material, historically often brass but more often plastic or acrylic today, which have a pattern cut into their edge that makes the distance of edge of the rosette from its center variable.
<p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="NewRosette" class="size-full wp-image-122 aligncenter" height="403" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="NewRosette" width="498"/></p>
Currently you can create a small number of rosettes using the interface above.
<h4>Circular Rosette</h4>
This rosette is not interesting at all. It just returns a constant radius from the centre of the spindle. Is single parameter allows you to specify its radius.

<h4>Polygonal Rosette</h4>
This is a Rosette in the form of an N sided polygon. You must specify the radius (the distance of all vertices from the centre) and the number of sides. The individual sides can be either; convex, straight or concave dependent upon the value of a scale factor which is either negative, zero, or positive. The magnitude of the scale factor affects the magnitude of the deflection from straight.<a href="{{ '/software/ornamental-turning-workbench/rosette/polygonal-rosette/' | relative_url }}" target="_self">See Examples</a>

<h4>Wave Rosette</h4>
The perimeter of this rosette is a sine wave.  You must specify the mean radius of the wave, its frequency, and amplitude<a href="{{ '/software/ornamental-turning-workbench/rosette/wave-rosette/' | relative_url }}"> see examples</a>. You can also select the absolute values to be true in which case you get the bottom half of the waveform (that part below the mean radius) reflected above the mean radius giving a set of  lobes that number twice the wave frequency.

<h4>EpiCycloid Rosette</h4>
An Epicycloid is created  by the path of a point on the diameter of a circle rolling around the outside of another circle. <h4>Hypocycloid Rosette</h4>
A Hypocycloid is created by the path of a point on the diameter of a smaller  inner circle rolling inside a larger circle. You give the radius of the outer circle as the Radius and the radius of the inner circle as the Rolling radius. If the radius is not an exact multiple of the Rolling radius then several full rotations may be required before the finishing point coincides with the starting point <a href=" {{ '/software/ornamental-turning-workbench/rosette/hypocycloid-rosette/' | relative_url }}" target="_self">see examples</a>.

<h4>Spiral Rosette</h4>
This Rosette has a radius that is a function of the angle it turns through. again specified by the scale factor which is the increase in radius per rotation. So a value of 1 will result in the radius being 360 units after one complete revolution. A value of 0.5 results in a radius of 180 units per revolution etc. This rosette is intended to be mainly used on the squash spindle to facilitate the cutting of helices etc.
