To cut spirals on a long(ish) piece you need to support the end furthest from the chuck. In order to allow this I have attached a Tailstock to the headstock of my machine, connected to it by an overhead tube. The tailstock as shown in the image below  will allow a piece up to 12 inches (300mm) in diameter and 12 inches (300mm) long to be supported.

The idea in this first version is that the supporting centre is pushed into the work by sliding the tube through its supports and then fastening it. If I find there is a need for some form of screw action to  position the support I will alter the bracket holding the centre.

<a href="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg"><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-976" height="344" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="IMG_0232" width="640"/></a>

Most parts are Aluminium bought from <a href="http://www.forwardmetals.co.uk/" target="_blank">forward metals</a> on Ebay, the verticals supports are 1 x 1.5 inch solid bar 250mm long.  The horizontal support tube is 30mm diameter with a 5mm wall thickness.    The boss fixed on the end of the tube is 50mm dia. I used Aluminium for  lightness and to match the rest of the machine. The only steel part is  the vertical shaft through the boss used to hold the centre and was a  bit  I had lying around salvaged from some equipment.

To hold  the revolving centre I salvaged an aluminium support bracket I no  longer used. This is the type of support bracket used to hold the track  bar that linear bearings run along (sold by Zapp Ltd, Marchant-Dice and  others here in the UK).  In this case the bore was 20mm diameter so I  machined a brass sleeve to fit in the bracket which  had a tapered  bore to fit an MT2 taper same as my other equipment.

<a href="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg"><img alt="" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-979" height="197" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="IMG_0232-2" width="300"/></a>

This fixture is attached on the end of a 16mm diameter steel rod which is in turn runs through the boss and is fixed  by a 10mm cap head bolt. The boss itself is bored to a tight fit on the  30mm tube and held solidly by a 5mm countersunk bolt screwed though the  boss and into the tube wall.

<a href="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg"><img alt="" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-980" height="225" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="IMG_0234" width="300"/></a>

The vertical supports sit in  slots machined in the headstock (not visible in this photograph)  and  their tops were bored to fit the 30mm diameter horizontal shaft which was then cut with a saw. The shaft is held tight using a compression fit provided by two m6 cap head bolts that act to pull the two sides of the bore together thus gripping the tube.

<a href="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg"><img alt="" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-981" height="291" src="/assets/placeholder-original-image.svg" title="IMG_0235" width="300"/></a>

I ultimately intend to replace (at some time) all the bolts with nice little machined screws but they will do for now.

The whole setup seems quite sturdy. Tugging on the tailstock tends to make the headstock flex on its supporting shafts, so I think it will be strong enough for its job.

<strong>Update</strong> after using it once to cut a helix pattern I think I will need some sort of screw thread to tighten centre into the piece.

Also due to the restricted movement along the pumping (Y) axis I can only cut helices of 150mm length in one continuous go.
